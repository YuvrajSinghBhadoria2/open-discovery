Josh — 9/16/26, 2:55 AM
Oh this is a question so near and dear to my heart! 

On some level everything is "just"  theorem proving. I think where things get dicey is the halting problem.

So rather than try to answer these lofty and provably unanswerable questions, we try to be fiercely pragmatic.

Our overall thinking boils down into precisely two abstractions manifested by configgle (priml being a user) and trackinizer.

In configgle-land, our thinking is that functions aren't actually the base cognitive abstraction but rather their arguments. Configgle therefore lifts arguments as first class citizens but also the "maker" and therefore supports dependency injection. This becomes very powerful for A/B testing, as evident from our results. It looks like others in ML have followed suit--torchtitan went this way roughly three weeks after I released configgle and in jax-land these ideas existed at apple since 2023.

Meanwhile in trackinizer our core axioms are:
it is impossble to make a universally ergonomic epistemology so instead lets focus only on provenance-- we'll be sure to track how it came to be rather than what it is
the exception to "only track how it came to be" (ie we do track what it "is") is when we want searchability. websearch exists because it needs webresults and paper exists because it needs authors. each Baconian inquiry exists only because we want specific edges

input pipeline throughput: take the imagenet exp000 data pipeline and let agents make it faster, with one hard rule: every batch has to come out bit-identical to the reference, so only speed can move. if a change alters a single pixel, the run fails and reports no number.

one trap i hit while building it: scored on wall images/sec, bumping decode threads from 2 to 8 alone gives ~1.7x with identical batches, so a swarm would "win" on day one just by adding threads. scoring on images per cpu-second (or pinning the core count) closes that.

exp000 is working locally: it times the pipeline exactly as the training exp000 runs it, and has a tensor golden. ~615 images/cpu-sec on my m4 pro over synthetic jpegs, but the real number should come from your hardware. on macs it needs #370 merged first.
